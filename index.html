About Me

My name is Tom Rongen, and I am a PhD Candidate in Economics at Maastricht University. I am supervised by Prof. Dr. Frederik Cörvers and Prof. Dr. Andries de Grip. My research is funded by Education Lab Netherlands and the Dutch Inspectorate of Education. My main focus is on teacher shortages in primary education. I exploit both existing administrative datasets and primary data to study how teacher shortages affect pupils and how shortages can be alleviated. My research is particularly focused on understanding teacher retention and the effects of improving working conditions. If you are interested in my research, feel free to contact me at tom.rongen@maastrichtuniversity.nl.

Thesis

Predicting School-Level Teacher Shortages

Read Full Abstract

Many countries face persistent teacher shortages, which are considered as a threat to educational quality. This highlights the importance of examining the prevalence of teacher shortages at the school level and identifying schools most at risk. Using administrative data on Dutch primary schools from 2016 to 2022, we construct school-level indicators for persistent teacher shortages and estimate a prediction model. Our findings show that schools with high proportions of specialized teachers, large number of temporary contracts, and high average experience are most at risk of developing persistent teacher shortages.

Teacher Shortages and educational (e)quality

Read Full Abstract

Educational systems worldwide have been faced with teacher shortages, raising concerns on the quality of education. While a substantial body of research has examined the consequences of teacher shortages, limited attention has been devoted to understanding how shortages affect educational quality. This paper examines how teacher shortages, measured at the school level, affect student achievements. Using administrative data from Dutch primary schools covering the period 2016-2023, we employ a within-school between-cohort design to examine the effect of teacher shortages on student learning outcomes in mathematics and reading.

The Effects of Improving Working Conditions on Teacher Turnover: Heterogeneous Responses by Work Stress Levels

Read Full Abstract

Teacher shortages remain a persistent challenge in Dutch primary education. Improving working conditions may help retain current teachers and attract former ones, but the effectiveness may vary based on individual preferences and stress levels. This study examines how teachers with different stress levels respond to improvements in working conditions using survey and administrative data. Understanding these heterogeneous responses can inform targeted policy interventions to reduce teacher turnover.

Preferences for Study Conditions at Teacher Education Programmes: Evidence from a Discrete-Choice Experiment

Read Full Abstract

This study uses a discrete-choice experiment to understand what aspects of teacher education programmes matter most to prospective teachers. We examine how various programme characteristics—such as work-study balance, practical experience, tuition costs, and career prospects—influence study choices. These findings can inform policy decisions aimed at making teacher education programmes more attractive to potential candidates.
